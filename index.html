Model Email Reply (Annotated) Standard Workplace Email

Dear Jason,

Thank you for your email about the Bread Runner Campaign. I am writing to make suggestions and explain how to coordinate the event.

Regarding the target participants, it might be a good idea to invite our colleagues and their families because they are easy to approach. Also, they are reliable and committed.

As for the promotional method, I suggest using the company's internal newsletters for two reasons. First, they can reach all colleagues directly. In addition, the preparation work is simple.

To coordinate this campaign, first, you should confirm the list of volunteers. Then, you should send event details such as locations and closing times of bakeries to the volunteers. The volunteers should arrive at bakeries 5 minutes before closing times. If the volunteers receive donations, pack the surplus bread. If there are no donations, you should record it on the activity report. Finally, hand over collected bread to charity organisations the day following the activity.

Hope this information helps. Please feel free to contact me if you have further questions.

Best regards,
Phoebe

Detailed Grammar Analysis & Rules Assessment Focus

1. Making Suggestions & Recommendations

In formal and semi-formal workplace emails, use polite phrases to offer advice to colleagues:

  • Pattern A: It might be a good idea to + base verb
    Example: It might be a good idea to invite our colleagues. (Never write "to inviting")
  • Pattern B: I suggest + gerund (-ing verb)
    Example: I suggest using the company newsletter. (Common error: I suggest to use)

2. Connectives & Giving Reasons

Support recommendations with logical reasons and extra supporting points:

  • because: Connects a main proposal directly with a reason clause. e.g., "...because they are easy to approach."
  • Also / In addition: Transitional adverbs used at the beginning of sentences to add supporting reasons. Follow with a comma. e.g., "In addition, the preparation work is simple."

3. Sequencers & Modals of Advice

Structure step-by-step procedures in work plans using clear signposts and modal verbs:

  • Sequencers (First, Then, Finally): Mark clear chronological order across multiple work plan tasks.
  • Modals of Advice (should): Express professional duties or advice.
    Rule: should + base verb (e.g., you should confirm). Never place "to" after should.

4. If-Conditionals & Imperatives

Give instructions for special conditions or alternative workplace scenarios:

  • If-Conditionals (Zero / Type 1 Conditionals): Used when actions depend on specific circumstances.
    Pattern: If + condition clause, + result / instruction clause.
  • Imperative Verbs: Give direct workplace instructions starting with base verbs.
    Examples: "pack the surplus bread", "hand over collected bread".
