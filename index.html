Annotated Model Reply Email
Suggestions Connectives Sequencers & Modals Conditionals Imperatives

From: Phoebe Chan <phoebechan@aec.com>

To: Jason Lee <jasonlee@aec.com>

Subject: Re: Bread Runner Campaign

Dear Jason,

Thank you for your email about the Bread Runner Campaign. I am writing to make suggestions for the target participants and promotional method, as well as to explain how to coordinate the campaign.

Regarding the target participants, it might be a good idea to invite our colleagues and their families, because they are easy to approach. Also, they are reliable and committed.

As for the promotional method, I suggest using the company's internal newsletters for two reasons. First, they can reach all colleagues directly. In addition, the preparation work is simple.

To coordinate this campaign, first, you should confirm the list of volunteers. Then, you should send event details such as locations and closing times of bakeries to the volunteers. The volunteers should arrive at bakeries 5 minutes before closing times.

If the volunteers receive donations, pack surplus bread. If there are no donations, you should record it on the activity report. Finally, hand over collected bread to charity organisations the day following the activity.

Hope this information helps. Please feel free to contact me if you have further questions.

Best regards,
Phoebe

Detailed Grammar & Language Analysis

1. Making Polite Suggestions

When giving ideas in workplace emails, polite structures avoid sounding bossy.

  • It might be a good idea to + base verb
    e.g., It might be a good idea to invite colleagues.
  • I suggest + -ing noun / verb
    e.g., I suggest using internal newsletters. (NOT "suggest to use")
2. Giving Reasons & Adding Points

Support your recommendations with clear logical connectives.

  • because + reason clause (answers "Why?")
  • Also, / In addition, + supporting point (adds a second reason)
3. Sequencers & Modals for Steps

Organise work procedures chronologically so instructions are easy to follow.

  • Sequencers: First, ... Then, ... Finally, ...
  • Modal Verb: You should + base verb (NO "to"! e.g., you should confirm)
4. If-Conditionals & Imperatives

Use conditional clauses to specify different outcomes based on practical situations.

  • If + situation, + imperative command
    e.g., If volunteers receive donations, pack surplus bread.
  • If + situation, + modal clause
    e.g., If there are no donations, you should record it on the report.
